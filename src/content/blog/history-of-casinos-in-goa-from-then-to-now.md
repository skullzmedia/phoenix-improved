## History of Casinos in Goa: From Then to Now

# Introduction
Goa, often called the ‘Las Vegas of India,’ has a unique and fascinating history when it comes to casinos. Today, it’s the one of the very few states in India  where legal casinos thrive. But this glamorous industry didn’t develop overnight—it took years of policy changes, tourism growth, and cultural shifts. Let’s explore how the casino industry in Goa has evolved, from its beginnings to its current status as one of India’s top entertainment destinations.

# The Early Days: Gambling Before Casinos
Long before Goa had official casinos, gambling existed informally in many parts of India. Card games like Teen Patti, Rummy, and Flush were already popular among locals, especially during festivals like Diwali. However, formal gambling establishments were banned under the Public Gambling Act of 1867, which extended into post-independence India.

In Goa, things changed in 1976 when the state amended the Public Gambling Act to allow ‘lottery and slot machines’ in five-star hotels. This was the first stepping stone toward creating a structured gambling industry in the state.

# The Birth of Offshore Casinos (1990s)
The real transformation came in the 1990s. In 1992, Goa allowed electronic slot machines in luxury hotels. This was soon followed by a landmark moment in 1999, when the first live offshore casino—Casino Goa—was launched on a yacht in the Mandovi River. It offered table games like Roulette and Blackjack, making it a huge attraction for domestic and international tourists.

This move was revolutionary because land-based casinos were still restricted to slot machines, but offshore casinos could offer live dealer games. This distinction created two types of casinos in Goa: land-based (slots) and offshore (live gaming).

# Rapid Expansion in the 2000s
With tourism booming in Goa, the 2000s saw rapid growth in the number of offshore casinos. Popular names like Deltin Royale, Deltin Jaqk, Casino Pride, and others entered the scene. These floating casinos became iconic, offering not just gambling but full-scale entertainment with restaurants, bars, and live shows.

By the late 2000s, Goa was firmly on the map as India’s casino capital. Tourists from Mumbai, Delhi, Bangalore, and even abroad began flocking to the state for its nightlife and gaming culture.
